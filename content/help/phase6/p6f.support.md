<!-- inline -->
A passage from outside your main text can help, but it costs time and attention. Add one only when it earns its place, and keep it visually subordinate so it never competes with the passage you are studying.

<!-- expandable -->
There are three kinds, and they behave differently. Context is an earlier part of the same book that today's text needs; it usually becomes your framing or first question. Quoted or alluded is where the author has sent you himself; it must be printed and framed as a question. Background is needed for understanding but not for discussion; it becomes a box, never a question. Each support passage costs about 3 to 5 minutes, so the tool warns you at the third of the context or quoted kind. The main passage is the subject; a support passage is a servant to it.
