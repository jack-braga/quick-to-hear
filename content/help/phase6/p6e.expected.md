<!-- inline -->
Before this question can join the study, write the answer you expect. It is the one thing the tool requires. If you cannot say what a good answer looks like, the question is not ready, and your group will feel it.

<!-- expandable -->
This is the only hard rule in the tool; everything else is a warning you can override. The expected answer is the fastest test of whether a question works. Writing it forces three things at once: that the question really has an answer in the text, that the answer is worth the time it will take, and that you will know a good answer when you hear one and can move the group on. Questions that fail this are usually vague ("What do you think about grace?"), or they are really you fishing for the words already in your head. If the expected answer comes out thin, the test has done its job: cut the question or sharpen it. It never appears on the participant handout. It is for you.
