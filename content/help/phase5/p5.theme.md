<!-- inline -->
The theme is what the passage says, in one sentence. Not its topic (prayer, suffering) but its point: what the passage actually claims. Fill in the frame: *In this passage, [author] shows that ___.*

<!-- expandable -->
A topic is a word; a theme is a sentence. "Suffering" is a topic. "Suffering does not mean God has abandoned his people" is a theme, because it makes a claim you could agree or disagree with. Aim for one sentence that captures the single main point, sometimes called the big idea. If your sentence would fit three other passages equally well, it is probably a topic or a truism rather than this passage's theme, so push it until it needs this text. It helps to pan back to the context here: seeing where the passage sits in the book shows you what the author is pressing.
