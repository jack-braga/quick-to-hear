<!-- inline -->
Say in one sentence how this passage gets to Christ. Follow the route rather than jumping straight there: text → its place in the Bible's unfolding story → its fulfilment in Christ → us, who are in him.

<!-- expandable -->
This is a distinct step, not part of the theme, and it is here for a reason. A guide like this one is good as far as it goes, but on its own it can assume you already see how a passage fits the whole Bible's story, or skip the question altogether. This step is where we refuse to skip it. *— after Goldsworthy*

The route matters more than the destination. Do not reach for Christ by resemblance, and do not tack the cross onto the end. Trace the line the passage itself sits on: a promise, a covenant, an office, an institution, an unsolved problem. Follow that line forward to him, and then to us in him. This does not mean a full lecture on biblical theology every time. It means knowing the connection is real and letting it shape the study. If the link is genuinely faint, that is worth knowing too, so say so rather than forcing it.
