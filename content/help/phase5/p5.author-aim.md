<!-- inline -->
The aim is what the author wanted to happen: not what the passage says, but what he wrote it to do to his first readers. Fill in the frame: *He wrote it so that his readers would ___.*

<!-- expandable -->
Theme and aim are a pair, and they are different. The theme is the point; the aim is the effect, the change the author wanted in the people who first received it. Ask what he wanted them to know, feel, or do differently by the end. Keep it to the original readers for now; you will turn it toward your own group in the next step. A passage written to reassure frightened believers is doing something different from one written to warn complacent ones, even when the theme sounds similar, and the aim is what tells them apart.
