Now turn the author's aim toward your group, and let it grow out of his rather than out of whatever struck you most. Given what he wanted his first readers to become, what do you want for the people in the room?

<!-- expandable -->
The temptation is to lead with what moved you, or with the need you already had in mind for the group. But if your aim does not grow out of the author's, you are no longer teaching this passage; you are using it. Start from the author's aim and ask how it lands on these particular people: their pressures, their stage, what they are tempted to believe or ignore. The passage sets the destination; your knowledge of the group shapes the road in. If you cannot draw a straight line from the author's aim to yours, the problem is usually the group aim, not the passage.
