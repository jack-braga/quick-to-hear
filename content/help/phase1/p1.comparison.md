<!-- inline -->
Comparing translations shows you where translators had to make a decision. It is for noticing those choices, not for picking the wording you like best.

<!-- expandable -->
When two faithful translations differ, it often marks a place where the original can be carried into English in more than one way, and that is worth seeing: it is a point where meaning is being weighed. Bring a comparison in to notice that, and to ask why they differ. What it is not for is shopping around until you find the version that says what you already wanted to say. If you find yourself preferring a rendering because it suits your point, that is the moment to stop and stay with your primary text.
