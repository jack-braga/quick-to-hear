Getting the sections right is worth more time than it looks. Follow the author's own breaks, the turns in his argument or story, rather than the chapter and verse numbers, which were added centuries later and often fall in the wrong places. Name each section in your own words. If you cannot name it, you may not have found its edges yet.

Two mistakes cause most of the trouble later. Cutting mid-argument, stopping partway through a single unit of thought, leaves you with half a point. Bundling, treating two units as one, leaves two points fighting for one theme. Either way, when you reach Phase 5 and cannot find a single theme, come back here first. A surprising share of "I'm stuck" is really a boundary problem rather than a thinking problem. Fix the edges and the theme often falls out on its own.
