**The Christ connection and its traps.** The step that asks how a passage gets to Christ, and the traps it guards against, follow the biblical theology of Graeme Goldsworthy, *Preaching the Whole Bible as Christian Scripture* (Eerdmans, 2000). ## Further reading

These are books this tool's tradition rests on, worth reading if you want to go further. Listing a book here does not mean the tool's content came from it; most of what you read here is the tool's own. Where we have drawn on a book directly, it says so.

- **David Helm, *One-to-One Bible Reading* (Matthias Media).** Drawn on directly. The COMA method and its genre question sets. The simplest way in to reading the Bible with one other person.
- **Graeme Goldsworthy, *Preaching the Whole Bible as Christian Scripture* (Eerdmans).** Drawn on directly. Why every text is read in the light of the whole Bible and its fulfilment in Christ, and the traps of forgetting it. His shorter *Gospel and Kingdom* is a gentler start.
- **Richard Sweatman, *Writing a Small Group Study*.** The most directly on-point book for what this tool is for: turning a passage into good questions.
- **Colin Marshall, *Growth Groups* (Matthias Media).** Training small-group leaders to teach the Bible, not only to keep a discussion going.
- **Colin Marshall and Tony Payne, *The Trellis and the Vine* (Matthias Media).** The case for word ministry and for training people, the tradition this tool's training use belongs to.
- **Phillip Jensen and Paul Grimmond, *The Archer and the Arrow* (Matthias Media).** Preaching a passage's own point, a good companion to the theme-and-aim step.
- **Haddon Robinson, *Biblical Preaching*.** Finding the single controlling idea of a passage and its purpose.
- **Bryan Chapell, *Christ-Centered Preaching*.** The Fallen Condition Focus: reading for the human need a passage speaks to.
- **Gordon Fee and Douglas Stuart, *How to Read the Bible for All Its Worth*.** How genre changes the way you read, and how to find where a passage begins and ends.
- **Charles Simeon, *Horae Homileticae* (preface).** A classic on letting the text set the aim. Public domain.
- **William Perkins, *The Arte of Prophecying*.** An early Protestant manual on opening and applying Scripture. Public domain.

You may want to add the writers who shaped this tradition more broadly; this list keeps to the books most useful for the task of preparing a study.
