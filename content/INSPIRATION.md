## 2. Theological & pedagogical inspiration (sources and where they land)

### Sources held (read at some point in the project)
- **ACS, *Preparing a Small Group Study*** (the owner's church training document) — study-prep
  disciplines; the movements-of-thought / big-idea framing; sketch answers; prioritise and drop;
  application as corporate and think/speak/act; pray before and again after. **Ships uncited**
  (owner's decision: good content, no church named).
- **David Helm, *One-to-One Bible Reading*** — the COMA grid (Context, Observation, Meaning,
  Application) and the six genre question sets. **Verbatim by permission** (Matthias Media / HTC).
- **Graeme Goldsworthy, *Preaching the Whole Bible as Christian Scripture*** — biblical theology;
  the text-to-Christ route; the traps (moralism, Christless preaching, the predictable "Jesus
  bit"); gospel before law; and the self-criticism of inductive study guides. **Cited** where used.
- **Colin Marshall, *Growth Groups*** — training leaders as teachers of the Bible, not only
  facilitators; question craft.
- **Colin Marshall & Tony Payne, *The Trellis and the Vine*** — word ministry and training; the
  conviction the tool's training use belongs to.

### Sources named / held-back (not held; recommended, for a later citation sweep)
- **Richard Sweatman, *Writing a Small Group Study*** (Matthias Media) — the single most on-point
  book for the task; question craft.
- **Haddon Robinson, *Biblical Preaching*** — the single controlling idea of a passage plus its
  purpose (the "big idea" family).
- **Bryan Chapell, *Christ-Centered Preaching*** — the Fallen Condition Focus; the "deadly be's".
- **Gordon Fee & Douglas Stuart, *How to Read the Bible for All Its Worth*** — genre and the
  literary unit.
- **Phillip Jensen & Paul Grimmond, *The Archer and the Arrow*** — preaching a passage's own
  point (theme and aim).
- **Graeme Goldsworthy, *Gospel and Kingdom*** — a gentler entry to his biblical theology.
- **Charles Simeon, *Horae Homileticae*** (preface) — letting the text set the aim; public domain.
- **William Perkins, *The Arte of Prophecying*** — opening and applying Scripture; public domain.
- **(Reformed lineage for the indicative-imperative:** Herman Ridderbos, Herman Bavinck.)

### Key ideas and where they live in the tool
- **COMA** (Context / Observation / Meaning / Application) → Phase 4 (Helm; verbatim sets).
- **Biblical theology / the Christ route** (text → its place in the covenant story → fulfilment
  in Christ → us in him) → `p5.christ-route`, `home.philosophy` (Goldsworthy, cited).
- **The four traps** (moralism, allegory, Christless history, flattening) → `traps.yaml` (the
  trap *concept* follows Goldsworthy; the four-way table and checks are the tool's own; allegory
  is the tool's own).
- **Theme and author's aim; know / feel / do** → Phase 5 (the tool's own framing; big-idea family
  = Robinson; movements of thought = ACS).
- **Faithfulness is not certainty** → `p5.faithfulness` (the tool's own; the reassurance that
  matters most).
- **The one hard rule: an expected answer** → `p6e.expected` (discipline in ACS; the hard-block
  enforcement is the tool's own).
- **Question-generation formulas** (observation / meaning / context / application) → `formulas.yaml`
  (inductive-method family; Fee & Stuart; the tool's own wording).
- **Soft question warnings** (yes-no opener, leading, double-barrelled) → `warnings.yaml`
  (standard question craft; likely *Growth Groups* topic 6 / Sweatman).
- **Gospel before law / invert the indicative** → `formulas.yaml`, Phase 5 (Goldsworthy; a broad
  Reformed staple).
- **Fallen Condition Focus (resemblance only)** → `stuck-helpers` (what-is-it-against),
  `formulas.yaml` (locate-the-pressure) (Chapell; ships uncited).
- **Word ministry / "the struggle is the training"** → `home.philosophy` (Trellis & Vine, Growth
  Groups behind it; the framing is the tool's own).
- **The inductive-guide self-criticism** → `home.philosophy` (Goldsworthy, cited): the argument
  for why the theme-and-aim and Christ steps are mandatory, not optional.
